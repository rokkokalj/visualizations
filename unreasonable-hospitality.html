The Central Thesis

Service Is Black & White.
Hospitality Is Color.

Core Distinction

Two different things. One without the other is incomplete.

Service

Doing your job with competence and efficiency. Getting the right plate to the right person at the right table. It's the technical execution — the black and white.

Hospitality

Making people feel great about the job you're doing for them. Genuinely engaging with the person you're serving so you can make an authentic connection. It's the color.

Unreasonable Hospitality means pursuing hospitality with the same passion, attention to detail, and rigor that world-class chefs bring to food. No one who ever changed the game did so by being reasonable.

Framework #1

Restaurant-Smart vs.
Corporate-Smart

The best organizations are both. You need the creativity and autonomy of being restaurant-smart AND the systems and controls of being corporate-smart.

Restaurant-Smart

  • More autonomy and creative latitude
  • Greater sense of ownership
  • Nimble — fewer rules blocking connection
  • People feel empowered
  • Risk: lacks corporate infrastructure
  • Risk: leaves money on the table
vs

Corporate-Smart

  • Back-end systems and controls
  • Accounting, purchasing, HR infrastructure
  • More profitable through efficiency
  • Consistency across operations
  • Risk: control stifles creativity
  • Risk: people on the ground feel disempowered

The goal: Creativity is the main ingredient in striking a true balance. Corporate-smart doesn't have to stifle creativity — but left unchecked, it will.

Framework #2

The Rule of 95/5

Manage 95% of your budget down to the penny. Spend the last 5% "foolishly." It sounds irresponsible; in fact, it's some of the smartest money you'll ever spend.

95
Disciplined

Negotiate hard, cut waste, manage labor, monitor every penny.

5
"Foolish"

Splurge on the details that dramatically transform the experience.

Examples: Tiny Italian blue gelato spoons at MoMA. Grand Cru Burgundy as the final glass in wine pairings. Dom Pérignon staff parties. Brand-new sleds for a family who'd never seen snow. The 5% has an outsize impact because it's earned through the discipline of the 95%.

Framework #3

Building a Culture
of Excellence

Excellence isn't one grand gesture. It's the aggregation of marginal gains — thousands of details executed perfectly.

Principle

The One-Inch Rule

When you lose focus in the last inch, you compromise all the work done to get there. A reminder to stay present and follow through — literally (put plates down gently) and metaphorically (finish every task completely).

Principle

The Way You Do One Thing Is the Way You Do Everything

Place every plate so the manufacturer's stamp faces the guest. Unreasonable? Yes. But precision in the smallest details translates to precision in bigger ones. People can feel perfection.

Mantra

Their Perception Is Our Reality

If a guest says their steak is undercooked, the only response is "Let me fix it" — even if it's technically perfect. You're serving guests, not egos. Being right is irrelevant.

Mantra

Slow Down to Speed Up

Take 10 seconds to double-check an order, because entering the wrong dish can ruin your whole night. Strengthen the foundation before adding stories. Do less, and do it well.

Leadership

How to Lead People

The frameworks Will developed for building, managing, and inspiring teams.

01

Hire the Person, Not the Résumé

Look for people who run after strangers to return a dropped scarf. Hospitality is an instinct — you can teach wine knowledge, but you can't teach someone to care. Every hire sends a message to your existing team: surround A players with A players.

02

Praise in Public, Criticize in Private

Praise with emotion, criticize without emotion. Criticize the behavior, never the person. And if you can't find more compliments than criticism, that's a failure in leadership. Criticism is investment; praise is affirmation.

03

Find the Hidden Treasures

A leader's responsibility is to identify strengths, no matter how buried. The guy failing as a food runner became a genius expeditor. Don't fire people before checking if they're simply in the wrong role.

04

30 Minutes a Day Can Transform a Culture

A daily meeting where a collection of individuals becomes a team. Same time, same format, every day. Start with housekeeping, then inspiration. Tell them why, not just what. Fill their tanks before you ask them to fill others'.

05

Set Them Up to Succeed

Expectations too high = overwhelmed team. Cut what you're asking people to learn to digestible pieces. Make tests passable, not punitive. Your biggest responsibility: make sure people who are trying have what they need.

06

Promote Before They're Ready

The perfect moment to give someone more responsibility is before they're ready. Take a chance, and they'll work extra hard to prove you right. You can't promote from within if you wait until they have all the experience.

07

Leaders Say Sorry

The idea that you won't make errors is criminally stupid — as is thinking no one will notice. Publicly owning mistakes strengthens the bond with your team. If you're willing to criticize yourself, they'll accept criticism from you.

Framework #4

The Collaboration Engine

Collaboration was the foundation upon which Unreasonable Hospitality was built. Every single program improved by leaps and bounds.

System

Ownership Programs

Give people ownership of specific areas that match their passions. Beer geek runs beer. Coffee geek runs coffee. Budget + education + autonomy = programs that become best-in-class.

System

Strategic Planning for All

Close the restaurant. Invite everyone — from dishwashers to the GM — to brainstorm the future. A busser sees things a manager never can. Collective decisions create collective ownership.

System

Let Them Lead & Teach

Happy Hours led by staff. Saturday pre-meals run by team members. The more they taught, the more they learned. The more they led meetings, the more they became leaders.

Key Insight

Share the Spotlight

When press calls about your beer program, put them in touch with the person who runs it. Don't take credit for other people's work. The more mini-celebrities on staff, the longer the line of people wanting to work for you.

Key Insight

Great Leaders Create Leaders

You don't want a hundred keys; you win when you end up with only one — the key to the front door. The more responsibility you trust people with, the more responsible they become.

Framework #5

Choose Conflicting Goals

Hospitality and excellence are not friends. Embracing the tension between them — choosing "and" instead of "or" — forces innovation.

Goal A
Excellence
↔
Tension
Innovation
↔
Goal B
Hospitality

Easy hospitality without excellence

Who cares if the waitress forgot your Coke? What's a little sloppiness between friends? Warmth without precision.

Easy excellence without hospitality

Scare your staff into never making mistakes. Technical perfection, but they're living in fear — no warmth, no personality, no connection.

The hard path — and the only one that matters — is to be both. Multiple conflicting goals force you to innovate. This is what Roger Martin calls integrative thinking.

Southwest Airlines chose to be the lowest-cost airline AND #1 in customer and employee satisfaction. For 50 years, they've been the most profitable airline in the country.

Framework #6

Relationships Are Simple.
Simple Is Hard.

Rule

Don't Go to Bed Angry

Don't leave work harboring frustration toward a colleague. Talk things through. People usually want to be heard more than agreed with. Even without resolution, both feel lighter.

Strategy

Find the Third Option

When two passionate people get entrenched, try swapping sides. Argue for the other person's position. This jogs you out of "your" idea and often reveals a third option neither would have found alone.

Strategy

Concede the Win

Sometimes the only way forward: whoever cares more gets their way. "It's important to me" — that's all you need to say. But don't abuse the card. Willingness to relinquish builds trust.

Insight

Learn Their Tough-Love Language

One size fits one. Some people need gentle correction. Others won't hear you unless it comes with a little thunder. Know the people you're working with. Sarcasm, however, never works.

Framework #7

Restoring Balance

Ambition is a nuclear reactor. Left unchecked, it melts down. You can't pour endlessly from your own pitcher without stopping to refill it.

DBC

Deep Breathing Club

In moments of crisis, walk up to an overwhelmed colleague and say "DBC." A few deep breaths. What's really communicated: "I see you. We're in this together."

🖖

Touch the Lapel

Make eye contact with a colleague and touch your lapel = "I need help." Systemizing it stripped the stigma from asking. Asking for help is a display of strength.

⏳

Find Your Oxygen

Everyone's oxygen is different. Exercise, nature, cooking, friends, binge-watching TV. Put your own mask on first. You can only be restorative if you restore yourself.

The Big Move

Unreasonable Hospitality
in Practice

How to systematically create magic. Remove the transactional. Create Legends. Build a tool kit.

Step 1

Remove Everything Transactional

  • Remove the podium. Greet guests by name at the door (Google their photos beforehand). Welcome them like a friend, not a transaction.
  • Ban computers from the dining room. No printers, no screens. Build a separate room for them.
  • Ticketless coat check. Transfer coats to the on-deck room when a table is paying. Guests approach the door and their coats are already waiting.
  • Solve the check problem with generosity. Leave a full bottle of cognac at the table with the check. No one feels rushed when they've been given a bottle of free booze.
Step 2

Earn Informality

Start formal, then earn the right to be casual. Amp up formality at the beginning of the meal. Over the course of the evening, earn trust enough to shift expectations. Like calling your father-in-law "Mr. Tosi" until he tells you to call him Gino.

Being Present vs. Being Attentive: Attentive servers see everything and turn tables efficiently. Present servers are so focused on the interaction that the bonds they create are much stronger. The present servers earned more in tips despite serving fewer people.

Step 3

Create Legends

One-off, personalized gestures that give people a story to tell. The gift isn't the object — it's the story. It's proof you were seen and heard.

The Hot Dog

European tourists mentioned their only NYC regret: never having a street hot dog. Will bought a $2 Sabrett's, had Daniel plate it four ways. They called it the highlight of their entire trip.

The Snow Sleds

Spanish family's kids had never seen snow. EMP bought four sleds and had an SUV whisk them to Central Park after dinner for a nightcap in the fresh snow.

The $100 Grand Bars

Banker joked he needed a million dollars. They tucked a bag of ten 100 Grand chocolate bars under his chair. He called the night "legendary."

The Private Beach

Couple's vacation flight canceled. EMP turned the private dining room into a beach: chairs, sand, kiddie pool, tropical daiquiris with little umbrellas.

Step 4

Build a Tool Kit & Hire Dreamweavers

You can be proactive about improvisational hospitality. Pattern recognition: identify recurring moments, prepare materials in advance, empower your team to deploy them.

Tool Kit Examples

  • City maps with secret favorite spots for out-of-towners
  • Empire State Building tickets for excited tourists
  • Tiffany crystal flutes for newly engaged couples
  • Morning rescue kits (coffee, Alka-Seltzer, muffin) for guests anticipating hangovers
  • Airplane snack boxes for travelers heading to the airport
  • Plus One cards answering frequently asked questions

The Dreamweaver Role

A dedicated person (eventually a full team with their own studio) to execute personalized hospitality gestures. Santa's workshop: leather tools, sewing machine, art supplies. The best part: the team came up with ideas independently — the perfect marriage of ownership and improvisational hospitality.

Framework #8

Scaling a Culture

As you grow, you can't lose the very thing that gave you the opportunity to grow.

Method

Sourdough Starter

Transplant seasoned people from your existing team to seed the new spot with your culture. They communicate, through words and actions, everything you stand for. Their passion infects every new hire.

Method

Commit to Paper

Compile your core values, phrases, and stories into a little red book (Field Manual). Every company should spend weeks hashing out what they stand for and writing it down. Welcome new employees with the same warmth you welcome guests.

Insight

Creativity Is a Practice

Even McCartney had a system. Dispel the myth that creativity is spontaneous and limited to geniuses. Be disciplined and intentional about creating space to dream freely. No bad ideas at first.

Insight

Nobody Knows What They're Doing Before They Do It

The biggest accomplishments start with a simple commitment to do them. A black diamond is scary if you usually ski blues. Growth happens outside your comfort zone. Don't worry — you will get down the hill.

Cultural Language

Language Creates Culture

The shared phrases and mantras that shaped the culture at EMP — repeated daily until they became instinct.

"Make the Charitable Assumption"

Assume the best of people. Maybe they're dismissive because they just got divorced. They might need more hospitality than anyone else in the room.

"Constant, Gentle Pressure"

The kaizen principle — everyone should always be improving, getting a little better all the time. Never stop.

"Be the Swan"

Graceful neck and meticulous white feathers on the surface. Webbed feet churning furiously below, driving the glide.

"One Size Fits One"

Some guests love schmoozing. Others want you to disappear. Read the room. Serve them how they want to be served.

"Raindrops Make Oceans"

Chase every penny. Small cuts add up. Half a paper towel, one less linen cloth, cheaper toques. Discipline in the details.

"Adversity Is a Terrible Thing to Waste"

Don't panic in the face of adversity. Creativity is the better solution. The recession taught EMP more about profitability than any good year ever could.

Beyond Restaurants

Hospitality Exists in
Every Business

The principles apply everywhere. Look for inflection points and patterns in your business. When you find them, do something about it.

Real Estate

Stop leaving generic sparkling wine. If they mentioned doing yoga in the sunlit nook, leave a mat rolled out there. Remove the doorjamb where the seller marked her kids' heights. Total cost: $0. Value: priceless.

Car Sales

Buying a car for a new teenage driver? Give them a year of AAA ($119). Dad struggling with a booster seat? A bag of Goldfish and a DustBuster in the glovebox. That parent will never buy from anyone else.

Banking

You give private banking to your wealthiest clients. How much would it cost to give every teller a similarly attentive experience? How can you quantify the improvement in service they'd deliver?

Any Industry

You can sell houses, or sell homes. Provide financial services, or provide families with a future. Sell insurance, or offer the comfort of knowing loved ones are safe. Name for yourself why your work matters.

The key insight: It isn't the lavishness of the gift that counts, but its pricelessness. A $2 hot dog beat a bottle of vintage Krug. The value is in the thoughtfulness — proof that someone was seen.

The Final Lesson

Serve What You Want
to Receive

The restaurant changed radically over the years because Will and his team changed. Not for the sake of change itself, but because as they grew and evolved, what they wanted to receive changed too. In the end, they stripped away everything that wasn't essential and returned to first principles: "To be the most delicious and gracious restaurant in the world." Nine words. That's all they needed.
